02 / CAPABILITIES

What can EXCORDER do?

Live tools grounded in the capabilities implemented in the app.

IMG.01

Imaging & Range

See a live rear-camera view with azimuth, vertical angle, approximate center distance on supported LiDAR devices, and an RGB-derived simulated spectrum.

NAV.02

Location & Environment

View your position, coordinates, heading, GPS altitude, atmospheric pressure, relative altitude, and local weather context.

MAG.03

Magnetic Field

Explore three-axis magnetic-field readings, total field strength, vector azimuth and elevation, plus a live 3D projection. Field strength is measured in µT (microteslas), where 1 µT equals one millionth of a tesla.

MOT.04

Motion & Vibration

Follow 100 Hz three-axis acceleration and inspect a 0–50 Hz vibration spectrum with peak frequency and amplitude.

AUD.05

Acoustic Analysis

Visualize relative microphone level in dBFS and a live 32-band spectrum from 40 Hz to 16 kHz, with adjustable digital boost.

VOC.06

Voice Decode

Transcribe Japanese, English, Spanish, Korean, German, or French live, with translation on supported iOS versions.

* Availability and accuracy vary by iPhone model, iOS version, permissions, conditions, and sensor support. EXCORDER is for observation and reference—not safety-critical or calibrated professional measurement.

03 / SENSORS

iPhone Sensors Used by EXCORDER

SENSOR / 01

Camera & LiDAR

The rear camera shows a live view with heading and device vertical angle. On supported LiDAR-equipped iPhones, EXCORDER also displays approximate distance at the center of the image. The color spectrum is simulated from camera RGB data; it is not a physical spectrometer reading.

SENSOR / 02

Magnetometer & Compass

Inspect three-axis magnetic field readings, total strength in µT, vector direction and a 3D projection. Compass heading helps you orient the camera and navigation views.

SENSOR / 03

GPS & Barometer

See your position on a map, coordinates and GPS altitude. The barometer provides air pressure and relative altitude changes. Local temperature and humidity come from the Open-Meteo weather service, not from built-in temperature or humidity sensors.

SENSOR / 04

Accelerometer & Device Motion

Follow three-axis acceleration sampled at 100 Hz and a 0–50 Hz vibration spectrum with peak frequency and amplitude. Core Motion combines motion sensor data, including gyroscope input, for device orientation used in the vertical-angle and magnetic-vector displays.

SENSOR / 05

Microphone

View relative microphone level in dBFS and a 40 Hz–16 kHz audio spectrum. Adjustable digital boost helps inspect quieter signals. These are relative digital audio levels, not calibrated sound pressure measurements.

04 / HARDWARE

Built from the sensors already in your iPhone.

EXCORDER didn’t begin in 2026.

It began around 1973, in Hokkaido, Japan.

I was about nine years old.

One day, by chance, I saw Star Trek on television.

The first scene I remember is Kirk and Spock playing three-dimensional chess.

Then Spock talks about humans — and smiles, just a little.

I was fascinated by Spock.

Science.
Logic.
Computers.

Measuring the unknown, gathering data, and using it to find answers.

“I want to do something like this someday.”

That thought, from a child watching television in Hokkaido, became one of the first steps that eventually led me into the world of computers.

Nearly half a century passed.

Computers, electronics, sensors, communications.

I have spent my life building things along the path that began with what fascinated me that day.

Then came 2026.

I looked at what was already inside an iPhone — cameras, LiDAR, a magnetometer, accelerometers, GPS, a barometer, microphones, and more.

And I thought:

Maybe I can finally build that futuristic instrument I dreamed about as a child.

So I did.

That became EXCORDER.

EXCORDER wasn’t born from market research.

It wasn’t built to follow a trend.

It is the result of a boy in Hokkaido, watching television around 1973, carrying that childhood fascination with him for more than half a century.
